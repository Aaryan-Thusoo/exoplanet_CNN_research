\subsection{Binary CNN Training History}

 The model trained over 10 epochs with no early stopping. During this time, the loss and accuracy of the training data was recorded at the end of each epoch. The validation data was not used in the updating of weights but was also applied at the end of each epoch and recorded as well. This was done to evaluate the improvements of the model over time to make sure to avoid training over too little or too many epochs. The final loss and accuracy results from the training data was \textbf{(Fill that in)}. The validation data ended at \textbf{(Fill that in)} and \textbf{(Fill that in)} respectively. The results throughout the epoch are recorded to check learning progress and overfitting.
 
Figure~\ref{fig:bi_history} shows line plots of the training and validation results over the epochs. Loss decreases over the first epochs and begins to stabilize showing the model starts to learn the patterns but eventually hits a rough limit on its performance. The accuracy increases over the first epochs but also stabilizes showing improvements in the classification. The flattening of the curves suggest adding more epochs likely will not strongly improve performance. As in both cases the training and validation lines have the same shape and do not spread far apart from one another, it is clear to conclude the model has not suffered from noticeable over fitting. If by the final epoch, the training and validation lines were heavily separated, this would indicate the model was learning individual features and not the general features that separate transits from eclipsing binaries.

\subsection{Binary CNN Test Performance}

Pipelines using higher or lower epochs led to a consistent trend of over or under fitting cases. After the completion of the training, the model was given the test data to determine the networks quality. The test data was never seen by the model throughout the training and was now useful for evaluating the models ability to detect general patterns made by each class. In total there were \textbf{(How many?)} test instances used with there being an equal split of transits and eclipsing binaries. After the evaluation of the model, the most important metrics were saved and are recorded below in Table~\ref{tab:metrics_table}. 

\begin{table}[]
    \centering
    \begin{tabular}{c|c}
        Accuracy & 0.85 \\ \hline
        Precision & 0.78 \\ \hline
        Recall & 0.96\\ \hline
        F1 Score & 0.86\\ \hline
        ROC AUC & 0.89
    \end{tabular}
    \caption{The above table shows the final metrics of the model. All scores are fine but for use on new data the results need to be improved to increase reliability.}
    \label{tab:metrics_table}
\end{table}

First, the accuracy shows the model correctly classified about $85\%$ of all the test light curves. Overall this is a strong performance indicating the model could be useful in further research. On its own however, accuracy is not enough to confirm the model performs equally for both classes. This is why the precision and recall metrics were required. A precision of $78\%$ means most of the instances the binary model classified as a transit were actual transiting exoplanets. However a good number of them were incorrectly labelled and $22\%$ of them were false transit detections. The recall of $96\%$ means the model barely missed any of the true transits. While this is good it may be due to the model predicting more instances in general as transiting exoplanets. 

The F1 score of $0.86$ works as a balance of the precision and recall. The result in the table depicts a model that maintains a good balance between recovering true transits and avoiding false transit detections, although precision remains the weaker component. Finally, the ROC AUC result of $0.89$ is strong and thus the model separates classes well over all possible thresholds. There is however still room to try slight variations in the threshold to potentially improve the model further. 

A confusion matrix is also helpful evaluating the model results. It efficiently displays the values of the true and false positives and negatives. Figure~\ref{fig:acc_conf_matrix} shows the confusion matrix and depicts how well the model performed. By looking at the figure, it matches to the metrics observed earlier. \textbf{(What percent?)}\% of eclipsing binaries were incorrectly classified compared to only \textbf{(What percent?)}\% of transits. It is shown in clearer view how the model missed many more eclipsing binaries than transits and thus the network prefers to call instances as transits in more cases than not.

\subsection{Binary CNN Confidence and Misclassification Analysis}

Of the \textbf{(How many total?)} test instances used, \textbf{(How many misclassified?)} were misclassified. There were \textbf{(How many false positives?)} false positives and \textbf{(How many false negatives?)} false negatives and to understand where the model is making mistakes it is important to analyse the properties of these light curve instances. The first feature to analyse was the distribution of confidence for all test instances. Classifications with low confidence indicate the model was not making an informed prediction and guessed. Misclassified instances with high confidence are the most interesting focus as the model assigns high confidence to its prediction. 

Figure~\ref{fig:conf_corr_vs_incorr_hist} shows the confidence distribution of all test instances split by correct and incorrect classifications. With any model, especially in a binary classification model, it is expected to see incorrect instances with low confidence. Thus it is important to analyse the incorrect cases which came back with higher confidence values.

To understand where the model is getting high confidence misclassification from, it is helpful to look at features of those light curve instances. The features chosen to be looked into are labelled in Table~\ref{tab:kep_query_cols} and were collected during the query steps in the Methodology. Apart from the \texttt{kepid} label and \texttt{koi\_disposition}, the remaining columns all contain measured features of each light curve used. It is important to note that the calculated SNR developed in the methodology was added to this list for comparison against the \texttt{koi\_model\_snr}. Each of these features are plotted comparing their numbers to the confidence the model returned in its classification. By then looking trends it is possible to look for what features affect the model the most.

The first property evaluated is the signal-to-noise ratios. SNR was considered first as low SNR makes transit/eclipse signals harder to detect. As the data used in this model has not undergone any heavy filtering, binning, or cleaning steps it is expected the SNR values are smaller than other papers. To then first compare SNR values, this is done by splitting all misclassified instances into a low, medium, and high confidence ranges. All misclassified light curves were then plotted onto Figure~\ref{fig:calc_snr_boxplot} which shows the spread of SNR values for the three categories. As the values of SNR scale over large ranges, the y-axis is log-scaled. Figure~\ref{fig:calc_snr_boxplot_fp} shows all false positive cases and Figure~\ref{fig:calc_snr_boxplot_fn} is for the false negative cases.

First, in the false positive plot, there is a large spread of SNR values in the low and medium confidence ranges. Thus the misclassified results range across a wide range of calculated SNR values. It is difficult to come to strong conclusions about the high confidence range as few samples fall in that region for situations where the model labelled an eclipsing binary as a transit. Similarly, in the false negative case, the low confidence results are spread across several orders of magnitude of calculated SNR values as well. The medium and high confidence groups have SNR values covering the same range as the low confidence group. The calculated SNR may contribute to some errors in the model which can be seen in the low confidence group. However it does not seem that the calculated signal to noise ratio can explain the model's limitations by itself as high confidence errors occur across similar SNR ranges.
